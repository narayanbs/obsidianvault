Yes — there are a few **mental tricks** that can work surprisingly well, especially if your brain starts “trying to solve” the fact that you're awake.

 The key is to make the task **boring, low-effort, and non-goal-oriented**. Trying hard to fall asleep can itself keep you awake.

 ### 🧠 1. Random-word shuffling

 This is probably the closest to what you're asking about.

 Pick a letter, say **B**, and generate completely unrelated words:

 > boat → banana → bottle → blanket → bicycle → button → butterfly…

 After each word, briefly picture it, then move on. **Don't make a story.**

 When you run out, switch letters.

 The randomness seems useful because it occupies enough of your language/visual system to stop you from ruminating, without being interesting enough to keep you alert.

 ### 🔢 2. The “boring countdown”

 Count backwards from 300 by 3:

 > 300, 297, 294, 291…

 If you lose your place, **don't restart and don't get annoyed**. Just pick up somewhere approximately.

 You can make it even less effortful: imagine each number appearing on a black screen and slowly fading away.

 ### 🐑 3. Category + letter

 Choose a category and alphabetize it in your head:

 > Animals: ant → bear → cat → dog → elephant…

 Or make it harder:

 > Foods beginning with C: cake → carrot → cheese → cereal…

 If you can't think of the next one, that's fine. **The struggling itself can make you sleepy.**

 ### 🌊 4. “Tiny movie” technique

 Imagine something extremely repetitive and slow:

 - walking slowly along an empty beach
- folding towels
- watching rain hit a window
- driving down a familiar road at night
- floating in a pool

 Don't construct a plot. Just let little fragments appear.

 ### 😴 5\. The paradoxical trick

 Instead of:

 > _“I need to fall asleep.”_

 try:

 > _“I don't need to sleep. I can just lie here and rest.”_

 Keep your eyes closed and give yourself permission to remain awake.

 The important part is **not turning this into another technique you have to perform correctly**.

 ### One particularly good combination

 If I woke up tonight, I'd try:

 **Random words → whenever I get distracted, return to random words → no checking the time.**

 For example:

 > candle… mountain… spoon… rabbit… window… orange… ladder… cloud…

 Let the images become fuzzy. If you suddenly realize you've been thinking about something else, that's actually fine — just start another word.

 And if you're awake long enough that you're becoming frustrated, it's generally better to **stop trying to force sleep**; briefly getting up and doing something quiet in dim light can be more useful than lying there fighting it.
